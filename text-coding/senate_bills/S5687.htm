                                 A BILL


 
  To codify the process for petitions for executive clemency, and for 
                            other purposes.

    Be it enacted by the Senate and House of Representatives of the 
United States of America in Congress assembled,

SECTION 1. SHORT TITLE.

    This Act may be cited as the ``Presidential Pardon Integrity Act''.

SEC. 2. DEFINITIONS.

    In this Act:
            (1) Covered benefit.--The term ``covered benefit'' means 
        anything of value, including any contribution, donation, gift, 
        service, payment, transfer, contract, investment, goods, 
        business opportunity, or other benefit, whether direct or 
        indirect, provided by a covered recipient, at the direction of 
        a covered recipient, or on behalf of a covered recipient, with 
        an aggregate value of not less than $5,000.
            (2) Covered recipient.--The term ``covered recipient'' 
        means a person who is granted executive clemency.
            (3) Executive clemency.--The term ``executive clemency''--
                    (A) means any exercise by the President of the 
                power to grant reprieves or pardons described under 
                clause 1 of section 2 of article II of the Constitution 
                of the United States; and
                    (B) includes any pardon, commutation, reprieve, or 
                remission of a fine granted by the President.

SEC. 3. OFFICE OF THE PARDON ATTORNEY.

    (a) Establishment.--
            (1) In general.--There shall be in the Department of 
        Justice an Office of the Pardon Attorney, headed by a Pardon 
        Attorney who shall assist the President in the exercise of the 
        power under clause 1 of section 2 of article II of the 
        Constitution of the United States.
            (2) Personnel matters.--
                    (A) Classification.--The position of Pardon 
                Attorney shall be a Senior Executive Service position 
                designated as a career reserved position under section 
                3132(b) of title 5, United States Code, and may be 
                filled only by a career appointee.
                    (B) Reporting.--The Pardon Attorney shall report 
                directly to the Deputy Attorney General.
                    (C) Additional personnel.--The Deputy Attorney 
                General may appoint such additional officers and 
                employees of the Office of the Pardon Attorney as the 
                Deputy Attorney General determines necessary.
    (b) Duties.--Under the general supervision of the Attorney General 
and the direction of the Deputy Attorney General, subject to subsection 
(c), the Pardon Attorney shall conduct, handle, or supervise--
            (1) the powers and functions under section 4; and
            (2) such other duties as may be assigned by the Attorney 
        General or the Deputy Attorney General.
    (c) Recommendations.--The Pardon Attorney shall submit all 
recommendations in clemency cases through the Deputy Attorney General 
and the Deputy Attorney General shall exercise such discretion and 
authority as is appropriate and necessary for the handling and 
transmittal of such recommendations to the President.

SEC. 4. PROCESSING OF PETITIONS FOR EXECUTIVE CLEMENCY.

    (a) Submission of Petition.--
            (1) In general.--A person seeking executive clemency by 
        pardon, reprieve, commutation of sentence, or remission of fine 
        shall execute a formal petition. The petition shall be 
        addressed to the President of the United States and shall be 
        submitted to the Pardon Attorney, Department of Justice, 
        Washington, DC 20530, except for petitions relating to military 
        offenses.
            (2) Forms.--The Pardon Attorney and the wardens of Federal 
        penal institutions shall make available forms for petitioning 
        for executive clemency and other required forms.
            (3) Military offenses.--A petitioner applying for executive 
        clemency with respect to a military offense shall submit his or 
        her petition directly to the Secretary of the military 
        department that had original jurisdiction over the court-
        martial trial and conviction of the petitioner. In such a case, 
        a form furnished by the Pardon Attorney may be used but should 
        be modified to meet the needs of the particular case.
            (4) Contents of petition.--Each petition for executive 
        clemency shall include the information required in the form 
        prescribed by the Pardon Attorney.
    (b) Offenses Against the Laws of Possessions or Territories of the 
United States.--A petition for executive clemency shall relate only to 
violations of laws of the United States. Petitions relating to 
violations of laws of the possessions of the United States or 
territories subject to the jurisdiction of the United States shall be 
submitted to the appropriate official or agency of the possession or 
territory concerned.
    (c) Consideration of Petitions; Notification of Victims; 
Recommendations to the President.--
            (1) In general.--Upon receipt of a petition for executive 
        clemency, the Pardon Attorney shall cause such investigation to 
        be made of the matter as he or she may determine necessary and 
        appropriate, using the services of, or obtaining reports from, 
        appropriate officials and agencies of the Government, including 
        the Federal Bureau of Investigation.
            (2) Notification of victims.--
                    (A) In general.--When a person requests clemency 
                (in the form of either a commutation of a sentence or a 
                pardon after serving a sentence) for a conviction of a 
                felony offense for which there was a victim, and the 
                Pardon Attorney concludes from the information 
                developed in the clemency case that investigation of 
                the clemency case warrants contacting the victim, the 
                Pardon Attorney, with assistance from the United States 
                attorney for the district of conviction or, if a 
                litigating component of the Department of Justice was 
                responsible for the case, the Assistant Attorney 
                General in charge of the component, shall make a 
                reasonable effort to notify each victim of the crime 
                for which clemency is sought--
                            (i) that a clemency petition has been 
                        filed;
                            (ii) that the victim may submit comments 
                        regarding clemency; and
                            (iii) whether the clemency request 
                        ultimately is granted or denied by the 
                        President.
                    (B) Considerations.--In determining whether 
                contacting the victim is warranted, the Pardon Attorney 
                shall consider the seriousness and recency of the 
                offense, the nature and extent of the harm to the 
                victim, the defendant's overall criminal history and 
                history of violent behavior, and the likelihood that 
                clemency could be recommended in the case.
                    (C) Mandatory notice.--In the case of a victim who 
                has on file with the Federal Bureau of Prisons a 
                request to be notified of an offender's release from 
                custody pursuant to section 551.152 of title 28, Code 
                of Federal Regulations, or any successor regulation, 
                the Pardon Attorney shall notify the victim of the 
                release.
                    (D) Reasonable efforts.--The Pardon Attorney shall 
                be deemed to have made reasonable effort to notify a 
                victim if the Pardon Attorney, with assistance from the 
                United States attorney for the district of conviction 
                or, if a litigating component of the Department of 
                Justice was responsible for the case, the Assistant 
                Attorney General in charge of the component, mails, or 
                directs the mailing of, written notice to the last-
                known address reported by the victim to the Federal 
                Bureau of Prisons under section 551.152 of title 28, 
                Code of Federal Regulations, or any successor 
                regulation.
                    (E) Definition.--In this paragraph, the term 
                ``victim'' means the following individuals:
                            (i) An individual who has suffered direct 
                        or threatened physical, emotional, or pecuniary 
                        harm as a result of the commission of the crime 
                        for which clemency is sought.
                            (ii) In the case of an individual who died 
                        or was rendered incompetent as a direct and 
                        proximate result of the commission of the crime 
                        for which clemency is sought, one of the 
                        following relatives of the victim (in order of 
                        preference):
                                    (I) The spouse.
                                    (II) An adult child.
                                    (III) A parent.
            (3) Review and recommendation.--The Pardon Attorney shall 
        review each petition and all pertinent information developed by 
        the investigation and shall determine whether the request for 
        clemency is of sufficient merit to warrant favorable action by 
        the President. The Pardon Attorney shall report in writing his 
        or her recommendation to the President, stating whether in his 
        or her judgment the President should grant or deny the 
        petition.
    (d) Notification of Grant of Clemency.--When a petition for 
executive clemency is granted, the Pardon Attorney shall--
            (1) in the case of a grant of a pardon, notify the 
        petitioner or his or her attorney of such action and mail, or 
        direct the mailing of, the warrant of pardon to the petitioner;
            (2) in the case of a grant of a commutation of sentence, 
        notify the petitioner of such action and deliver the warrant of 
        a commutation to the petitioner through the officer in charge 
        of his or her place of confinement, or directly to the 
        petitioner if the petitioner is on parole, probation, or 
        supervised release; and
            (3) in all cases of a grant of executive clemency, not 
        later than 30 days after the date on which the President grants 
        the individual executive clemency, post on a public website 
        maintained by the Department of Justice the name of the 
        individual granted clemency, the district of conviction, 
        sentence, offense, and reason for the grant of executive 
        clemency.
    (e) Notification of Denial of Clemency.--
            (1) In general.--Whenever the President notifies the Pardon 
        Attorney that the President has denied a request for clemency, 
        the Pardon Attorney shall so advise the petitioner and close 
        the case.
            (2) Recommendation of denial.--Except in cases in which a 
        sentence of death has been imposed, whenever the Pardon 
        Attorney recommends that the President deny a request for 
        clemency and the President does not disapprove or take other 
        action with respect to that adverse recommendation within 90 
        days after the date of its submission to the President, it 
        shall be presumed that the President concurs in that adverse 
        recommendation of the Pardon Attorney, and the Pardon Attorney 
        shall so advise the petitioner and close the case.
    (f) Delegation of Authority.--The Pardon Attorney may delegate to 
any officer or employee of the Office of the Pardon Attorney any of his 
or her duties or responsibilities under this section.
    (g) Disclosure of Files.--
            (1) Construction.--Nothing in this section shall be 
        construed to limit or otherwise affect the disclosure under 
        section 552 of title 5, United States Code (commonly known as 
        the ``Freedom of Information Act'') of any petition, report, 
        memorandum, or communication created, submitted, or furnished, 
        in whole or in part, in connection with the consideration of a 
        petition for executive clemency, including the recommendation 
        of the Pardon Attorney to the President.
            (2) Submission to congress.--
                    (A) In general.--Not later than 30 days after the 
                date on which the President grants an individual 
                executive clemency, the Pardon Attorney shall submit to 
                the chairman and ranking member of the Committee on the 
                Judiciary of the Senate and the Committee on the 
                Judiciary of the House of Representatives all 
                petitions, reports, memoranda, and communications 
                created, submitted, or furnished, in whole or in part, 
                in connection with the consideration of the petition 
                for executive clemency, including the Pardon Attorney's 
                recommendation to the President.
                    (B) No records.--If there are no petitions, 
                reports, memoranda, or communications described in 
                subparagraph (A) with respect to a grant of executive 
                clemency to an individual, the Pardon Attorney shall 
                submit to the chairman and ranking member of the 
                Committee on the Judiciary of the Senate and the 
                Committee on the Judiciary of the House of 
                Representatives a certification so indicating.

SEC. 5. COLLECTION OF INFORMATION REGARDING GRANTS OF EXECUTIVE 
              CLEMENCY.

    (a) Notice to Covered Recipients.--Not later than 2 days after the 
date on which the President grants a covered recipient executive 
clemency, the Office of the Pardon Attorney, in coordination with the 
Bureau of Prisons, shall notify the covered recipient that the covered 
recipient is required to submit information in accordance with 
subsection (b).
    (b) Information.--Not later than 62 days after the date on which 
the President grants a covered recipient executive clemency, the 
covered recipient shall submit, through the online portal established 
under subsection (d), the following information:
            (1) Whether the covered recipient engaged another person or 
        entity to lobby for clemency and the identity of such person or 
        entity, if applicable, including engaging another person or 
        entity to provide legal services rendered in connection with 
        representation before a court of law, lobbying, advocacy, 
        advice or other services related to the seeking or obtaining 
        executive clemency.
            (2) Whether the covered recipient, or someone on behalf of 
        the covered recipient, provided a covered benefit to--
                    (A) the President or an immediate family member of 
                the President, including the spouse of an immediate 
                family member;
                    (B) a member of the Cabinet or an immediate family 
                member of a member of the Cabinet;
                    (C) a Member of Congress or an immediate family 
                member of a Member of Congress; or
                    (D) any entity established, financed, maintained, 
                or controlled by the President or an immediate family 
                member of the President, including the spouse of an 
                immediate family member.
    (c) Public Notice.--Not later than 62 days after the date on which 
the President grants a covered recipient executive clemency, the Office 
of the Pardon Attorney shall post on a public website the information 
collected pursuant to paragraphs (1) and (2) of subsection (b).
    (d) Online Portal.--Not later than 90 days after the date of 
enactment of this section, the Attorney General shall establish an 
online portal through which covered recipients shall submit the 
information required under subsection (b) and section 6.
    (e) Enforcement.--A covered recipient who knowingly fails to submit 
information required to be submitted under subsection (b), submits 
materially false or incomplete information under subsection (b), or 
otherwise violates this section shall be subject to a civil penalty of 
not more than $5,000.
    (f) Rule of Construction.--Nothing in subsection (b)(1) shall be 
construed to limit any State common law privilege, including the 
attorney-client privilege.

SEC. 6. DISCLOSURE BY PERSONS WHO SEEK EXECUTIVE CLEMENCY ON BEHALF OF 
              OTHERS FOR COMPENSATION.

    (a) Definitions.--In this section:
            (1) Clemency contact.--The term ``clemency contact'' means 
        any oral or written communication, including an electronic 
        communication, to a covered individual that is made with regard 
        to a request for executive clemency or the grant or denial of 
        executive clemency to a specific person, without regard to 
        whether a petition has been submitted under section 4.
            (2) Clemency representative.--
                    (A) In general.--The term ``clemency 
                representative'' means any person or entity that, for 
                compensation, makes a clemency contact on behalf of 
                another person, without regard to--
                            (i) whether the person or entity is 
                        registered under the Lobbying Disclosure Act of 
                        1995 (2 U.S.C. 1601 et seq.);
                            (ii) the number of clemency contacts made 
                        by the person or entity; or
                            (iii) the proportion of time the person or 
                        entity devotes to clemency contacts.
                    (B) Exclusion.--The term ``clemency 
                representative'' does not include any individual, 
                organization, or other entity, or any officer employee, 
                member, or volunteer of an organization or entity, that 
                makes a clemency contact on behalf of another person 
                without receiving compensation, directly or indirectly, 
                from or on behalf of that person for the clemency 
                contact.
            (3) Compensation.--The term ``compensation'' means any 
        payment, fee, retainer, contingent or success fee, gift, loan, 
        investment, business opportunity, or other thing of value, 
        whether provided or promised, directly or indirectly.
            (4) Covered individual.--The term ``covered individual'' 
        means--
                    (A) the President;
                    (B) the Vice President;
                    (C) any officer or employee of the Executive Office 
                of the President;
                    (D) any officer or employee of the Office of the 
                Vice President;
                    (E) the Attorney General;
                    (F) the Deputy Attorney General;
                    (G) any officer or employee of the Office of the 
                Attorney General;
                    (H) any officer or employee of the Office of the 
                Deputy Attorney General;
                    (I) any officer or employee of the Office of the 
                Pardon Attorney;
                    (J) any other covered executive branch official, as 
                defined in section 3 of the Lobbying Disclosure Act of 
                1995 (2 U.S.C. 1602); or
                    (K) an immediate family member of the President, 
                including the spouse of an immediate family member.
    (b) Disclosure Required.--
            (1) Initial disclosure.--Not later than 30 days after the 
        date on which a clemency representative first makes a clemency 
        contact on behalf of a person, the clemency representative 
        shall submit to the Pardon Attorney, through the online portal 
        established under section 5(d), a disclosure containing the 
        information described in subsection (c).
            (2) Supplemental disclosure.--A clemency representative 
        shall submit through the online portal established under 
        section 5(d) an updated disclosure containing any information 
        described in subsection (c) not included in a prior disclosure 
        not later than 30 days after--
                    (A) the date on which the President grants or 
                denies executive clemency to the person on whose behalf 
                the clemency representative made a clemency contact; 
                and
                    (B) the date on which the clemency representative 
                receives or is promised any compensation not reported 
                in a prior disclosure.
    (c) Contents.--Each disclosure under subsection (b) shall include--
            (1) the name, address, and principal place of business of 
        the clemency representative and, in the case of a clemency 
        representative that is an entity, the name of each employee of 
        the entity who made a clemency contact;
            (2) the name of the person or persons on whose behalf each 
        clemency contact was made;
            (3) the name of each person or entity that paid, promised, 
        or arranged compensation to the clemency representative, if 
        different from the person described in paragraph (2);
            (4) for each clemency contact, the date and form of the 
        communication and the name and position of each covered 
        individual contacted;
            (5) the nature, amount, and terms of all compensation 
        received or promised, including whether any portion of the 
        compensation is contingent on a grant of executive clemency; 
        and
            (6) whether the clemency representative, or any person 
        acting on behalf of the clemency representative, provided a 
        contribution, donation, gift, service, payment, or other thing 
        of value with an aggregate value of not less than $5,000 to a 
        person or entity described in subparagraphs (A) through (D) of 
        section 5(b)(2) during the 3-year period ending on the date of 
        the disclosure, and, if so, the recipient, amount, and date of 
        each such provision.
    (d) Public Notice and Submission to Congress.--Not later than 15 
days after the date on which the Pardon Attorney receives a disclosure 
under subsection (b), the Pardon Attorney shall--
            (1) post the disclosure on the public website described in 
        section 5(c) in a searchable, sortable, and downloadable 
        format; and
            (2) notify the chairman and ranking member of the Committee 
        on the Judiciary of the Senate and the Committee on the 
        Judiciary of the House of Representatives of the disclosure.
    (e) Penalties.--
            (1) Civil penalty.--A clemency representative that 
        knowingly fails to comply with this section, including 
        knowingly omitting information required under subsection (c), 
        shall be subject to a civil penalty of not more than $200,000.
            (2) Referral.--The Pardon Attorney shall refer any apparent 
        violation of this section to the Attorney General and, not 
        later than 30 days after making the referral, shall notify the 
        chairman and ranking member of the Committee on the Judiciary 
        of the Senate and the Committee on the Judiciary of the House 
        of Representatives of the referral.
    (f) Rules of Construction.--Nothing in this section shall be 
construed to--
            (1) require the disclosure of the content of any 
        communication protected by the attorney-client privilege, 
        except that the identity of a clemency representative, the fact 
        and date of a clemency contact, and the compensation described 
        in subsection (c)(5) shall not be considered privileged for 
        purposes of this section; or
            (2) exempt a clemency representative from any obligation 
        under the Lobbying Disclosure Act of 1995 (2 U.S.C. 1601 et 
        seq.) or the Foreign Agents Registration Act of 1938, as 
        amended (22 U.S.C. 611 et seq.).

SEC. 7. PRIVATE RIGHT OF ACTION FOR RESTITUTION.

    (a) Definitions.--In this section:
            (1) Covered restitution awardee.--The term ``covered 
        restitution awardee'' means a person who is the beneficiary of 
        an order of restitution entered before the date of a grant of 
        executive clemency to a covered recipient with respect to an 
        offense for which the covered recipient received executive 
        clemency.
            (2) Order of restitution.--The term ``order of 
        restitution'' means an order providing for restitution--
                    (A) under section 3663 or 3663A of title 18, United 
                States Code;
                    (B) under any other statute authorizing criminal 
                restitution; or
                    (C) as applicable at common law.
    (b) Right of Action.--
            (1) In general.--A covered restitution awardee may, upon a 
        failure of a covered recipient to pay amounts due to the 
        covered awardee under an order of restitution, bring a civil 
        action in an appropriate district court of the United States 
        seeking such payment.
            (2) Jurisdiction.--The district courts of the United States 
        shall have original jurisdiction of a civil action brought 
        under paragraph (1).
    (c) Relief.--In a civil action under subsection (b), the court may, 
without regard to the receipt of executive clemency by a covered 
recipient, and upon a failure by the covered recipient to pay the 
restitution amount, award a covered restitution awardee the amount of 
the restitution provided for under the applicable order of restitution 
and costs, including reasonable attorneys fees.

SEC. 8. RULE OF CONSTRUCTION.

    Nothing in this Act shall be construed to limit the power of the 
President to grant executive clemency under the powers granted by 
section 2 of article II of the Constitution of the United States.
